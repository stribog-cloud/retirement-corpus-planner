# 2026-07-06 v2.0.0 Release Notes

## What changed for users

- **Dynamic withdrawal rules.** Beyond the existing fixed, inflation-indexed cash target, you can now choose Guardrails (a simplified Guyton-Klinger rule that cuts spending when the withdrawal rate drifts too high, raises it when the rate drifts too low, and otherwise holds a year's inflation increase flat after a market loss) or % of corpus (the yearly target is recomputed as a fixed share of that year's opening corpus). An optional spending floor protects a minimum monthly cash level under either rule. Fixed remains the default and is unchanged.
- **Multi-goal planner.** The household plan can now hold up to 10 named one-time lump-sum goals — a car, a wedding, a renovation — each with its own amount, landing year, and inflation setting, instead of a single planned lump sum.
- **Historical Backtest Lab.** Simulations gains a new Backtest Lab card that replays every rolling window of bundled, approximate India market history (fiscal years 1990-91 through 2024-25) through the same live cash engine Monte Carlo uses, instead of randomly sampled paths. It reports success rate, the worst and best starting fiscal year, and P10/P50/P90 bands, with an option to replay each historical window's own year-by-year inflation. Read it alongside Monte Carlo, not instead of it — it is a reality check against one specific, approximate history, not audited performance data or a forecast.
- **Automatic capital-loss carry-forward.** A capital loss realised during the projection window is now carried forward automatically for up to 8 assessment years and applied against future gains under the SWP and Interest cash engines, following the short-term-first, then long-term ordering the law requires. There is no toggle for this — it corrects a previously unmodelled tax benefit. Plans that never realise a loss are unaffected.
- **Opt-in tax-aware rebalancing.** The SWP engine's annual equity/debt rebalancing can now optionally realise capital gains or losses on the selling leg instead of moving value tax-free, so a rebalance participates in the same year's tax and carry-forward accounting. This stays off by default; the previous tax-free rebalancing behaviour is unchanged when it is off.
- **Trust and platform improvements.** The app now follows your system's light/dark theme preference in addition to the manual toggle, dialogs and drawers have more consistent keyboard and focus behaviour, and the zero-network guarantee is now checked automatically on every change in addition to the local build check. Each GitHub Release now ships a `SHA256SUMS` checksum file alongside the HTML download — see the [Verify your download](../../../README.md#verify-your-download) section in the README.

## Do I need to do anything?

No. Existing saved plans and scenarios load and behave exactly as before — the default withdrawal rule stays Fixed, tax-aware rebalancing stays off, and a previously saved single planned lump sum migrates automatically into the new multi-goal editor as one entry. There is nothing to re-enter or re-save.

## Planning-grade caveats

- The Historical Backtest Lab's bundled dataset is a planning-grade approximation, not audited performance data — see its in-app disclosure and the [Risk, Simulations, and Heatmap Colours](../concepts/risk-simulations-and-heatmap.md) guide before treating its output as a forecast.
- Dynamic withdrawal rules, multi-goal planning, and tax-aware rebalancing change what the model targets or realises; they do not change the product's overall scope as a planning aid rather than filing-grade tax advice or regulated investment advice.

## What did not change

The product remains local-first. It does not send assumptions to a server and does not emit telemetry. It is still a planning aid, not filing-grade tax advice, regulated investment advice, or a guarantee of retirement outcomes.

## Recommended action

- Open Assumption Studio > Risk & Goals to review the new withdrawal-rule options, even if you plan to keep Fixed.
- Try the Historical Backtest Lab in Simulations alongside your existing Monte Carlo results.
- Review the household tab's Planned lump-sum goals editor if you previously used a single planned lump sum.
- Regenerate PDF, CSV, and review-pack exports after reviewing these settings, so exported evidence matches the current plan.
